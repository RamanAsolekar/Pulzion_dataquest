Sulfuric acid concentrations were estimated at a boreal forest site using a proxy based on global radiation, SO 2 concentration and condensation sink. The proxy explained 78 % of the variance in measured H 2 SO 4 during daytime, but strongly underestimated concentrations at night when radiation is close to zero. We therefore tested a modified proxy that includes ozonolysis of monoterpenes as an additional source term. Including the alkene term improved nighttime agreement only marginally, suggesting that an unidentified oxidant contributes to H 2 SO 4 production after sunset. The coefficients were fitted with nonlinear least squares using two summers of measurements, and the fitted exponent for the condensation sink was close to unity, consistent with previous studies in Hyytiala.
OH radical concentrations were measured with chemical ionization mass spectrometry during a summer campaign in a polluted megacity. Measured OH peaked at 1.2 10 7 molecules cm [][-3] around noon and remained above 10 6 molecules cm [][-3] at night. Model simulations constrained by observed VOCs underestimated OH by a factor of two under high NO x conditions, a discrepancy attributed to missing recycling mechanisms. The photolysis frequency of ozone explained most of the diurnal variability of OH. These results highlight that radiation-based parameterizations of OH may fail in urban environments with strong nocturnal chemistry.
The COVID-19 lockdown in early 2020 provided a natural experiment to evaluate the response of PM 2 5 to emission reductions in the North China Plain. In Beijing, PM 2 5 concentrations decreased by only 17 % during the lockdown despite a 70 % reduction in traffic flow, because enhanced secondary aerosol formation under stagnant meteorology offset primary emission reductions. Nitrate fractions increased from 18 % to 27 % of PM 2 5 mass, driven by higher atmospheric oxidation capacity. Positive matrix factorization identified secondary nitrate, secondary sulfate, biomass burning and dust as the main factors. Biomass burning contributions increased during the Spring Festival because of fireworks and residential heating.
Wintertime haze episodes in a semi-arid basin city were characterized using online aerosol chemical speciation monitor measurements. Organic aerosol dominated submicron particle mass during haze, accounting for 45 %, followed by sulfate and nitrate. Low wind speeds and a shallow boundary layer favoured accumulation of pollutants, while dust events in spring increased coarse particle concentrations. Source apportionment of organic aerosol identified coal combustion, cooking and oxygenated organic aerosol factors. The results suggest that controlling residential coal combustion in winter is essential for improving air quality in semi-arid regions of northwestern China.
Total column water vapour from reanalyses and satellite retrievals was compared over the Indian Ocean during the monsoon onset. ERA5 showed a dry bias of 1 to 2 kg m [][-2] relative to microwave radiometer retrievals over the Arabian Sea, while MERRA-2 was wetter than both. The discrepancies were largest in regions of deep convection, where retrieval algorithms are affected by precipitation contamination. Decomposing the water vapour field into dynamic regimes using mid-tropospheric vertical velocity showed that the differences arise mostly in ascending regimes. Climate models from CMIP5 overestimated water vapour in subsidence regions.
Sudden stratospheric warmings are frequently followed by persistent negative phases of the Northern Annular Mode near the surface. Using 60 years of reanalysis data, we composited 38 major warming events and examined their impact on wintertime air pollution in eastern China. Following warming events, surface wind speeds decreased and boundary layer heights were reduced, leading to increases in PM 2 5 concentrations of about 10 % over the following 30 days. The downward propagation of stratospheric anomalies was evident in about two thirds of events. Seasonal forecast systems that resolve the stratosphere showed improved skill in predicting these pollution anomalies.
Backward trajectories driven by ERA-Interim and ERA5 were used to reconstruct water vapour entering the stratosphere through the tropical tropopause layer. Trajectories were initialized daily on the 380 K isentropic surface and integrated backward for 60 days. The Lagrangian dry point temperature determined the final water vapour mixing ratio. Using ERA5 resulted in a moister lower stratosphere by about 0.5 ppmv compared to ERA-Interim, which was attributed to differences in the representation of temperature variability and vertical wind. Diabatic heating rates rather than kinematic vertical velocities were used, as they produce less dispersive trajectories.
Methane growth rates surged in 2020 and 2021 to the highest levels since systematic measurements began. Using an atmospheric inversion of surface observations, we attribute most of the increase to enhanced wetland emissions in the tropics associated with La Nina conditions, with a smaller contribution from reduced OH sink. Anthropogenic emissions from fossil fuel production were found to decrease slightly in 2020. The inferred wetland emission increase of 15 Tg yr [][-1] is consistent with process-based models driven by observed precipitation anomalies. The isotopic signature of atmospheric methane supports a predominantly biogenic source for the recent growth.
Ozone pollution during the 2020 lockdown in Wuhan was investigated using surface observations and a regional chemical transport model. Maximum daily 8 h average ozone increased by 36 % compared to the same period in 2019 because reductions of NO x emissions led to weaker titration in a VOC-limited regime. Satellite-derived HCHO/NO 2 ratios increased across central China, indicating a shift toward a transitional regime. Meteorological conditions were more favourable for ozone formation in 2020, contributing about one third of the observed increase. The results emphasize the need for coordinated NO x and VOC emission controls.
Atmospheric inversions of CO 2 fluxes over South Asia are limited by sparse in situ observations. We used a regional transport model at 10 km resolution to quantify transport errors associated with coarse global models. Errors in simulated boundary layer heights over the Indo-Gangetic Plain reached 40 %, leading to biases in near-surface CO 2 of up to 5 ppm at night. Assimilating column observations from OCO-2 reduced the posterior flux uncertainty over India by 30 %. The results suggest that high-resolution transport is essential for national-scale carbon budgets in regions with complex terrain.
Representation errors arise when point observations are compared with grid-box mean model values. We quantified representation errors in surface ozone over Europe using a 1 km resolution simulation aggregated to typical global model resolutions. Representation errors were largest near urban areas and point sources, exceeding 10 ppb at 100 km resolution, and were smallest over remote marine areas. Nighttime errors exceeded daytime errors because of the shallow boundary layer and the stronger influence of local emissions. These errors should be included in the observation error covariance used in data assimilation.
Ice crystal number concentrations in mixed-phase clouds were measured with a cloud particle imager and a holographic instrument during an aircraft campaign over the Southern Ocean. Shattering of large particles on probe tips was mitigated with antishatter tips and interarrival time filtering. Concentrations of ice crystals larger than 100 um were 1 to 2 orders of magnitude higher than predicted by primary nucleation parameterizations, suggesting active secondary ice production via rime splintering. Supercooled liquid water was identified using the King hot-wire probe. Size distributions were fitted with gamma functions for use in bulk microphysics schemes.
The Chemical Lagrangian Model of the Stratosphere was used to simulate the transport of tropospheric air into the Asian monsoon anticyclone. Air parcels were released from the boundary layer using emission tracers for different regions, and their transport was driven by ERA5 winds and diabatic heating rates. Approximately 1 million air parcels were used at a horizontal resolution of 70 km. Results show that India and southeastern Asia contribute most of the air reaching the anticyclone at 380 K. The tracer of air mass origin was compared with satellite observations of carbon monoxide from MLS.
Smoke emission coefficients relate fire radiative energy to emitted mass of aerosol and trace gases. We derived biome-specific coefficients for particulate matter over Africa by combining geostationary fire radiative power with aerosol optical depth retrievals from MODIS. Coefficients ranged from 15 g MJ [][-1] for grassland to 32 g MJ [][-1] for tropical forest. The resulting emission inventory agreed with bottom-up inventories within 25 % for the continental annual total, although agreement varied by region. Uncertainties are dominated by the conversion from aerosol optical depth to aerosol mass.
Carbon monoxide retrievals from the TROPOMI instrument were used to estimate fire emissions in Siberia during the 2019 fire season. Daily CO columns were fitted with a plume model to derive emission rates for large fire clusters. Retrieved emissions were compared with the Global Fire Assimilation System and found to be 40 % higher for boreal forest fires, potentially due to underestimated emission factors for smouldering combustion. Cloud contamination limited the number of usable overpasses to about half of the fire days. A stripe correction was applied to the TROPOMI data following the operational algorithm.
Pyrocumulonimbus events can inject smoke into the lower stratosphere, where solar heating of absorbing aerosol causes further ascent. Using lidar observations from the 2019 2020 Australian bushfires, we tracked a smoke plume rising from 16 km to 31 km over two months. A radiative transfer model coupled to a simple vertical transport scheme reproduced the observed ascent rates when the single scattering albedo was set to 0.8. The plume was associated with a synoptic-scale anticyclonic vortex that maintained its coherence. Ozone was depleted within the vortex by up to 30 %.
Volcanic eruptions inject SO 2 into the stratosphere where it is oxidized to sulfate aerosol. We simulated the 1991 Mount Pinatubo eruption with a global aerosol-climate model to investigate the sensitivity of stratospheric aerosol optical depth to the injected SO 2 mass. Injection of 14 Tg SO 2 overestimated observed optical depth by 40 %, while 10 Tg yielded better agreement. Aerosol microphysics, including coagulation and sedimentation, controlled the effective radius and the decay timescale. The model did not include the radiative effects of volcanic ash.
Volatile organic compounds were measured with a proton transfer reaction mass spectrometer at a high-altitude site in the Himalayas. Isoprene concentrations were highest in the afternoon during the monsoon season, reaching 0.5 ppb, consistent with emissions from vegetation on the southern slopes transported by valley winds. Oxygenated VOCs including methanol and acetone dominated the total VOC mixing ratio. Back trajectory analysis showed that polluted air from the Indo-Gangetic Plain reached the site during the pre-monsoon season. NO x concentrations were measured with a chemiluminescence analyser with a photolytic converter.
Iron solubility in mineral dust determines the supply of bioavailable iron to the ocean. We implemented a mineralogy-resolving dust scheme in an Earth system model and evaluated simulated iron deposition against cruise observations over the Atlantic. The model reproduced the observed decline of total iron concentrations with distance from the Sahara but underestimated soluble iron in remote regions. Atmospheric processing by acids increased iron solubility from 0.1 % at the source to 3 % over the remote ocean. Wet deposition dominated iron removal over the Southern Ocean.
Aerosol optical properties were measured at a rural site in the southeastern United States over a decade. Scattering coefficients were measured with an integrating nephelometer at three wavelengths and absorption coefficients with a particle soot absorption photometer. The scattering Angstrom exponent decreased during dust events from Africa in summer. Long-term trends showed a 40 % decline in scattering since 2005, consistent with reductions in SO 2 emissions. Single scattering albedo increased over the same period. Positive matrix factorization of filter-based chemical composition was used to attribute scattering to sources.
A positive matrix factorization model was applied to speciated PM 2 5 data from 12 sites in the Yangtze River Delta. The optimal solution with seven factors was selected based on Q/Qexp values and interpretability. Bootstrap analysis showed that all factors were reproducible in more than 95 % of runs. Secondary nitrate and sulfate factors together accounted for 48 % of PM 2 5, followed by vehicle emissions and industrial sources. Uncertainties in the factor profiles were evaluated with displacement analysis, and no factor swaps occurred.
Hygroscopic growth of aerosol particles was quantified with a humidified tandem differential mobility analyser at a coastal site. The growth factor at 90 % relative humidity ranged from 1.3 for organic-rich particles to 1.8 for sea-salt dominated particles. A scattering enhancement factor was derived by combining dry and humidified nephelometer measurements. The enhancement factor at 85 % RH averaged 1.9 and was strongly dependent on the inorganic mass fraction. These results were used to correct satellite-derived aerosol optical depth for ambient humidity effects.
The impact of lockdown measures on air quality in Shanghai in spring 2022 was assessed using surface monitoring data. PM 2 5 decreased by 30 % and NO 2 by 55 % relative to the previous three years, while ozone increased by 20 %. The weekly cycle of NO 2 disappeared during the lockdown, reflecting the suspension of commuting traffic. Meteorological normalization using a random forest model indicated that emission changes explained most of the reductions. Industrial emissions in the outskirts remained partially active, sustaining sulfate concentrations.
Satellite observations of XCO 2 from GOSAT and OCO-2 were used to detect urban CO 2 enhancements over megacities in Asia. Enhancements of 1 to 3 ppm were found over Delhi, Beijing and Tokyo relative to background values upwind. The detectability depended on wind speed and the number of clear-sky soundings. A Gaussian plume model was used to convert enhancements into emission estimates, which agreed with inventories within 20 % for most cities. Representation of topography in the retrieval was identified as a source of systematic error over mountainous terrain.
